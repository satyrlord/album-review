Timestamp-Based Structural Analysis

Oxygène 3

Artist: Jean-Michel Jarre
Released: 2 December 2016
Total Length: 39:54
Tracks: 7
Label: Sony · Columbia
Producer: Jean-Michel Jarre

Overview

Released on the 40th anniversary of Oxygène to the day, this third and final chapter was composed in six weeks and mixed entirely in Ableton Live — a deliberate choice Jarre describes as maintaining the minimalist constraint of the 1976 original, where an 8-track studio enforced discipline. The instrument list deliberately mirrors the earlier albums — Eminent 310, EMS Synthi AKS, ARP 2500/2600, Mellotron — alongside modern additions including Moog Sub 37, Teenage Engineering OP-1, Elektron Analog Keys and digital VSTs. Jarre described a "light and dark" architecture: the album moves from introspection toward resolution across its seven parts.

Oxygène (Part 19) was the compositional genesis: a piece written during the Electronica 1 sessions that made Jarre reconsider what the series could be in 2016. Part 20 directly samples wind and white-noise material from the original Oxygène Part VI, closing the trilogy with an explicit sonic quotation of its own origin. Parts 14–20 complete a sequence begun forty years earlier. Metacritic score: 65/100. UK Albums: No. 41; Switzerland: No. 14.

0:00 ARP 2600 sequences open — spare, familiar arpeggiation in the Oxygène register; the sonic handshake that connects three decades. The opening is almost archivally careful: the timbral match to the 1976 album is exact enough to feel like time has not passed.

2:00 Moog Sub 37 bass pulse — low-frequency rhythmic grounding, more felt than heard; anchors the composition without imposing percussive structure. The Sub 37 is the most contemporary element in this track; its presence is integrated rather than announced.
3:30 Filter intensification — resonant filter opens on the lead sequence, brightening the harmonic register.
4:50 Return to sparse opening motion — the filter closes, the bass withdraws; the track retreats to its original register. Structural arch mirroring the large-scale design of Part 20: everything comes back to where it started.
02 Oxygène (Part 15) 6:40
Energy: Low Wind Texture Long-Form Melody Lower Register
Album role: Expansive second piece — the album's most patient track; a long-form melodic statement in the lower register that establishes the "dark side" character fully before it migrates toward shade in Part 16.
0:00 Wind-noise texture opens — the same atmospheric device that bookmarks the trilogy; ARP enters cautiously over it. The wind noise is not ambient padding; it is the trilogy's recurring atmospheric signature, present in all three albums.
1:10 Long-form melodic statement in lower register — a broad, unhurried phrase that does not want to resolve quickly. The track's entire purpose is to let this melody breathe.
3:00 Textural density increase — Mellotron D4000 enters with string swells; the melody becomes embroidered rather than solo. The Mellotron D4000 is a digital Mellotron model; its character is softer than the M400 used on Oxygène 7–13 but retains the analog warmth.
4:30 Dark turn — harmonic shift into a minor mode deeper than the opening; the melodic material takes on an uncertain quality.
5:50 Gradual dissolution — layers recede, the wind texture reasserts, and the track fades without formal closure.
03 Oxygène (Part 16) 6:50
Energy: Low Dark Side Centrepiece EMS Synthi AKS Harmonic Ambiguity
Album role: Dark side centrepiece — the longest track and deepest point of the album's introspective arc; harmonic ambiguity never settles, reflecting the ecological anxiety Jarre cited as the album's conceptual underpinning.
0:00 Brooding slow-moving sequences — unhurried, low-pitched arpeggiation with wide spacing between events; space as tension.
1:35 EMS Synthi AKS melodic line — the instrument's distinctive portamento and timbral instability give the melody a slightly unstable, searching quality. The Synthi AKS is Jarre's most expressive instrument choice: its patch matrix means no two performances are identical.
3:20 Harmonic ambiguity peak — suspended chords and unresolved modal fragments accumulate; the track's "ecological warning" is clearest here. Jarre described the artwork and music as "an ecological warning signal, dark and surreal." This is the compositional correlate.
5:00 Gradual brightening — the harmonic texture begins to lift, filter opens, motion increases fractionally. The structural turn toward the "light side": Part 16 is the hinge between the album's two halves.
6:20 Return to opening texture — the track closes on the same sparse material that opened it, but the harmonic context has shifted slightly toward resolution.

04 Oxygène (Part 17) 4:20
Energy: Mid Lead Single Light Side Pivot OP-1 Accessible Hook
Album role: The light-side pivot and single — the album's most melodically direct piece; the tonal shift from the preceding three tracks is immediate and pronounced. Accompanied by a music video for its single release on 4 November 2016.
0:00 Warmer major-adjacent tonality — the harmonic register has shifted; the minor-mode weight of the dark side is absent. The tonal contrast against Parts 14–16 is the album's most structurally dramatic moment; a single heard without context misses it entirely.
0:40 Main melodic hook — the album's most immediately memorable phrase; designed for single format and broad accessibility.
1:50 Teenage Engineering OP-1 textural layer — distinctively modern timbre woven into the analog foundation; the only place on the album where the contemporary production is foregrounded. The OP-1's 4-track tape simulation and built-in synthesis give it a character recognisably different from the vintage instruments surrounding it.
3:00 Strip to bare sequences — brief exposure of the underlying material before the final reprise.
3:30 Full theme reprise — all elements return for the closing statement; the track ends decisively.
05 Oxygène (Part 18) 2:48
Energy: Low Interlude Palette Cleanser Minimal
Album role: Interlude and palette cleanser — the shortest piece on the album, functioning as a meditative suspension between the single and the closing two-part finale. Its brevity is its structural function.
0:00 Immediate minimal texture — reduced to a single melodic line and sparse pad; no introductory build. At 2:48, the shortest track on the album. Its function is decompression after Part 17's melodic density.
0:30 Single melodic line over sustained pad — one voice, no counterpoint; total structural simplicity.
1:45 Texture becomes fractionally more active — sequences enter briefly, suggesting the momentum of Part 19 is approaching.
2:30 Dissolve — the texture fragments and disappears, resolving nothing, opening everything.
06 Oxygène (Part 19) 5:45
Energy: Mid Genesis Track Original Atmosphere Pre-Finale
Album role: The compositional genesis of the entire album — the piece Jarre wrote during the Electronica 1 sessions that prompted the creation of Oxygène 3. Functions as the penultimate movement, restoring momentum toward the epic finale.
0:00 Sequences recalling original Oxygène atmosphere most directly — among all the album's tracks, this most closely mirrors the feel of the 1976 original. Its genesis explains this: written first and independently, before the album existed, without the burden of continuation. It is the most freely composed piece here.
1:05 Harmonic sequence development — RMI Harmonic Synthesizer enters with its distinctive timbral colour, bridging all three albums. The RMI Harmonic Synthesizer appears on all three Oxygène albums; its consistent presence is one of the trilogy's sonic through-lines.
2:30 Second arpeggiated layer added — counterpoint between two sequences increases the compositional density measurably.
4:05 Pre-climax build — all elements approach maximum density before truncation; the energy does not release, it accumulates into Part 20.
5:15 Dissolve into wind texture — atmospheric tail connects directly to the opening of Part 20.
07 Oxygène (Part 20) 7:58
Energy: Mid Trilogy Closer Samples Part VI (1976) Epic Finale Wind Noise Quotation
Album role: The trilogy's concluding statement — structurally the most complex piece, opening with a direct sample of Oxygène Part VI's wind and white-noise material and building to a full-scale finale that resolves sixty-three combined minutes of sequential music across forty years. The harmonic closure of 1976 is finally completed here.
0:00 Sampled wind and white-noise from Oxygène Part VI (1976) — the opening of this track is the closing of the original album, sounded again forty years on. This is not homage but suture: the exact audio material of 1976 is woven into the texture of 2016, collapsing the temporal distance between the two recordings.
0:35 1976 elements integrated into new sequence — the sampled material begins to interact with new synthesizer voices; history and present operate simultaneously.
2:05 New material builds over historical echo — the 2016 composition asserts itself fully; the sample becomes substrate rather than subject.
4:05 Full arrangement peak — the densest textural moment of the album; all instruments present, the trilogy's accumulated harmonic energy at maximum. The only point in Oxygène 3 that justifies comparison to the large-scale climax of Part IV on the 1976 album.
6:05 Harmonic resolution — the suspended harmonics of the preceding six tracks settle at last; a landing after forty years of open cadences.
7:05 Wind-noise return and final fade — the atmospheric signature of the entire trilogy resounds one last time; the music returns to breath and air. The structural circle is complete: wind opened the 1976 album, wind closes the 2016 album. Three records, forty years, one atmospheric signature.

Album Arc

Light / Dark Architecture

Jarre explicitly divided the album into a brooding "dark side" (Parts 14–16) and a warmer "light side" (Parts 17–20). The structural pivot is Part 17; its tonal shift is the album's most architecturally deliberate gesture.

Parts 14–16 · 17–20

Part 19 as Origin

Compositionally, the album began with Part 19 — written during Electronica 1 sessions in 2015. The album exists because this single piece made Jarre re-examine the Oxygène concept for 2016. The numbers do not reflect the creative chronology.

Part 19

Structural Quotation in Part 20

The direct sampling of Oxygène Part VI's wind and noise material in Part 20 is not nostalgic decoration but structural argument: the trilogy's ending contains its beginning, and the forty-year sequence is explicitly sealed.
