Tubular Bells III

Artist: Mike Oldfield
Released: 31 August 1998
Total Length: 46:36
Tracks: 11
Producer: Mike Oldfield

Overview

Tubular Bells III completes the trilogy — recorded partly in Oldfield's home studio at Es Cubells, Ibiza, and partly in London, it carries the Mediterranean warmth of its recording location throughout. The most vocal of the three Tubular Bells albums, featuring five distinct vocal artists including Irish folk singer Cara Dillon, Irish vocalist Clodagh Simonds, Moroccan singer Amar (credited on three tracks), and child voice Francesca Robertson. The album's closing track "Far Above the Clouds" explicitly references the Tubular Bells Part One finale and functions as a valediction. Released on 31 August 1998 — exactly six years after Tubular Bells II. UK #4. Performed live at Horse Guards Parade in torrential rain, 4 September 1998.

Drums on "Man in the Rain" are sampled from Oldfield's own 1983 hit "Moonlight Shadow." Timestamps approximate to ±4 seconds.

07 Man in the Rain 4:03
Energy: Mid Cara Dillon Vocal Irish Folk Sampled Drums: Moonlight Shadow
Album role: Cara Dillon's crystalline Irish folk voice over drums sampled from Oldfield's own 1983 hit "Moonlight Shadow" — a self-referential gesture connecting this album to its commercial predecessor. The song about a man standing in rain became the centrepiece performed at the Horse Guards Parade concert in actual torrential rain.
0:00 Drum pattern enters — recognisably derived from "Moonlight Shadow." The self-citation is deliberate. Oldfield sampling himself — closing a loop from 1983 to 1998. The drum feel is immediately familiar to fans of the earlier hit.
0:20 Cara Dillon vocal — lyrical, melodically precise Irish voice. Crystal quality. Dillon's vocal register is the highest on the album — pure, rained-on clarity.
1:30 Guitar and bass layer — arrangement fills out around Dillon's voice. Heather Burnett adds additional vocal texture in the fuller sections.
2:30 Full arrangement peak — the track's emotional summit. Voice soaring over full production. Ironic that this track about a man standing in rain was performed live in a literal rainstorm at Horse Guards Parade.
3:30 Rain-soaked fade — gradual dissolution. The man remains in the rain. The fade doesn't resolve tension — the rain continues beyond the track boundary.

11 Far Above the Clouds 5:30
Energy: High Trilogy Valediction Clodagh Simonds Vocal TB Part One Finale Reference Child Voice
Album role: The closing valediction of the entire trilogy — explicitly references the Tubular Bells Part One finale theme. Clodagh Simonds' soaring voice and child voice Francesca Robertson together carry the album's farewell. The album ends above the clouds, above all three records' accumulated complexity.
0:00 Ascending opening — rising melodic gesture, deliberately unambiguous in its upward direction. The final track declares its intent from bar one: ascension. The trilogy ends looking upward.
0:45 Clodagh Simonds vocal enters — soaring, ethereal. An Irish singer who worked with Van Morrison and others; here in her most elevated mode. Simonds' voice has a spiritual, ceremonial quality appropriate to the finale function.
1:45 Tubular Bells Part One finale theme — the original 1973 closing melody explicitly referenced. The trilogy makes contact with its own origin. A deliberate allusory gesture. In 1973 this theme meant "beginning"; in 1998, it means "I have come full circle."
2:30 Full production layers deploy — bass, guitar, orchestral pads, percussion all active. The album's most texturally complete arrangement moment — the culmination of all three albums' production logic.
3:30 Child voice — Francesca Robertson — enters above the full arrangement. The highest, purest voice on any of the three records. The choice of a child voice for the trilogy's final statement is an obvious but emotionally resonant decision. Innocence as perspective.
4:00 Climactic peak — all voices, full arrangement — the trilogy's final apex. Oldfield's guitar above everything. 25 years of Tubular Bells in a single moment of maximum compression. The resolution of three albums' worth of cumulative tension.
4:45 The ascent continues beyond the track — gradual fade upward. The album does not land; it goes higher. Ending in the sky rather than on the ground. The trilogy's final statement is an absence of closure — there is more sky above. Fittingly, the rain came down during the live premiere at Horse Guards Parade.

Macro Arc — Album Structure

Opening Block: Joy & Shadow

Tracks 1–5 introduce the album's vocal colours (Amar twice) and establish its tonal range — from Mediterranean warmth (Source of Secrets, Jewel in the Crown) to sinister shadow (Outcast, Serpent Dream).

Tracks 01–05 · ~20:01

Central Block: Folk & Voice

The album's vocal centrepiece — three distinct folk voices in succession: Rosa Cedrón (Galician), Cara Dillon (Irish), and the celebratory jig of Top of the Morning. The album's deepest human content.

Tracks 06–08 · ~13:10

Closing Sequence: Night to Sky

Moonwatch observes the night; Secrets gathers the energies; Far Above the Clouds ascends. The trilogy's culminating valediction — the only TB album to resolve its entire lineage in a single closing piece.

Tracks 09–11 · ~13:15

Vocal Architecture

Five distinct vocal artists — Amar (tracks 1, 3, 10), Rosa Cedrón (6), Cara Dillon (7), Clodagh Simonds (11), and child voice Francesca Robertson (11). More vocally diverse than TBII but no single Master of Ceremonies figure.
