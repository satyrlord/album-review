Tubular Bells II

Artist: Mike Oldfield
Released: 31 August 1992
Total Length: 58:34
Tracks: 14
Producers: Trevor Horn · Tom Newman · Mike Oldfield

Overview

Tubular Bells II arrived 19 years after the original — a deliberate sequel that honours its predecessor's architecture while applying high-budget 1990s production (Trevor Horn, the most technically perfectionist producer of the era). The album's 14 tracks abandon the original's continuous-suite format in favour of shorter, individually titled pieces, each named after or referencing Arthur C. Clarke short stories and concepts. Alan Rickman performs the Master of Ceremonies role in homage to Vivian Stanshall. The album reached UK #1 and sold 2 million copies — a reunion between Oldfield and the record-buying public that his experimental 1980s output never achieved.

Track titles reference Arthur C. Clarke stories: "Sentinel" (precursor to 2001: A Space Odyssey), "Sunjammer" (solar sail story), among others. Timestamps approximate to ±5 seconds.

Energy: High Epic Opener Arthur C. Clarke Ref. Multi-Phase
Album role: The opening statement — eight minutes that echo the original Part One's cumulative logic but in a darker, more cinematic production register. "The Sentinel" was Clarke's 1948 story that became 2001: A Space Odyssey.
0:00 Atmospheric opening — low synth drone and processed bell texture. Space music inheritance. Trevor Horn's production fingerprint is immediately audible — the low end is deeper and more controlled than the 1973 original.
0:45 Guitar motif introduces itself — a transformed version of the original Tubular Bells piano theme, now on guitar. Immediately recognisable but distinctly contemporary. Oldfield playing the famous cycle on guitar. The recontextualisation is the album's central gesture in its opening bars.
2:00 Bass and drums enter — a heavier, more produced rhythm section than the original's live feel. 1990s production values: tighter gate on snare, deeper sub bass, more precisely edited samples.
3:30 Orchestral elements introduced — string-pad textures and synthesised orchestral colours join. Horn's orchestral production expertise applied to Oldfield's instrumental palette.
5:15 First major peak — all elements converge. Tubular bells themselves strike. The actual bells are introduced earlier here than in the original — a knowing reversal of the 1973 deferred-entry strategy.
6:20 Breakdown — texture reduces to bell and atmospheric pad. Brief decompression before the final section.
6:50 Final build and resolution — guitar theme restated fully. Track ends in brightness rather than drama. The 8-minute opener closes on an affirmative note — contrast with the ominous drone that began it.

Album role: Folk elements reintroduce themselves — Oldfield's Celtic lineage resurfaces on Side 2, recalling the flageolet-based opening of Tubular Bells Part Two.

12 Altered State 5:12
Energy: High Live Drums John Robinson Drive
Album role: The album's most rhythm-forward track — John Robinson's live drum performance (the album's only significant outside contribution) drives the piece with extraordinary precision. The "altered state" of the title is tonal: the album's clearest departure from the progressive template.
0:00 Live drum kit launches immediately — John Robinson's performance is audibly human. The groove is warm and forward-leaning. Robinson brings a funk-session player's sensibility to the progressive context. Different feel from the rest of the album.
0:45 Oldfield guitar joins the rhythm section — riff-based guitar over the live drums. Rock feel. The combination of live drums and electric guitar riff is the closest the album comes to conventional rock structure.
2:00 Full arrangement peak — the track's core groove at maximum density. The album's most viscerally driving moment. Trevor Horn's production amplifies the live quality here rather than over-processing it.
3:30 Rhythmic breakdown — drums reduce; melodic elements sustain. ~20 seconds. The live feel is maintained even at reduced density.
3:50 Final drive — full groove restored for the closing passage. The track ends with the same forward momentum it opened with.
BPM: ~122 — Live-groove rock
13 Maya Gold 4:01

Album role: A warm, golden-register piece — the last melodic statement before the brief coda. The title's "gold" is expressed through the tonal warmth of the arrangement.

Album role: The album's closing coda — brief, luminous, and anchored by bagpipes from the Celtic Bevy Band / LAPD Pipe Band. A final salute before silence.

1:30 Final fade — the pipes fade to silence. The album ends. 58 minutes of music closes with Celtic stillness.

Macro Arc — Album Structure

Side 1: Construction

Seven tracks forming a coherent arc from Sentinel's opening through the interlude sequence (Dark Star to Red Dawn) to The Bell's ceremonial climax. Mirrors Tubular Bells (1973) Part One in macro shape.

Tracks 01–07 · ~30:06

Side 2: Exploration

Seven tracks in a different register — more intimate pieces alternating with higher-energy moments (Altered State with John Robinson's live drums). Resolved by Maya Gold's warmth and Moonshine's bagpipe coda.

Tracks 08–14 · ~28:28

Arthur C. Clarke Framework

Multiple track titles reference Clarke short stories — Sentinel (1948, basis for 2001), Sunjammer (1964), The Great Plain, Clear Light. The album exists in dialogue with science fiction's optimistic mode.

Multiple tracks

Production Context

Produced by Trevor Horn — the era's technician of maximum precision. His contribution is the sheer sonic scale of the 1992 recording, contrasting with the intimate Manor sessions of 1972. Performed live at Edinburgh Castle, September 1992.
