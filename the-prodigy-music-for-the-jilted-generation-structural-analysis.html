Purpose of This Document

A per-track, timestamp-level structural blueprint of "Music for the Jilted Generation." Each track is broken down into its arrangement events — when drums enter, when breakdowns happen, when energy peaks — so you can use the timings and structures as a skeleton for your own album. The album-arc section at the bottom maps the macro pacing across all 13 tracks.

Timestamps are approximate to ±2 seconds. BPM values are estimated. Energy tags rate each track's overall intensity ceiling.

01 Intro 0:46
Energy: Ambient Spoken Word Scene-Setter
Album role: Manifesto. Sets the political/emotional tone before a single beat drops.
0:00 Silence, then a low ambient hum fades in — filtered, cavernous reverb. Establishes negative space. No rhythm, no melody. Pure atmosphere.
0:06 Spoken-word vocal sample enters over the drone. Narrative/political tone — a voice addressing the listener directly. Dry vocal against wet ambient bed. Contrast is key.
0:30 Ambient bed begins to swell slightly, tension building beneath the final words. Very subtle rise in volume/density. No drums. The restraint here makes Track 2's entrance devastating.
0:42 Voice ends. Ambient trail fades into silence — brief gap before Track 2 detonates. ~2-3 seconds of near-silence. Critical for the impact of the transition.
BPM: N/A — No rhythmic content
02 Break & Enter 8:24
Energy: Peak Breakbeat Rave Epic
Album role: The opening assault. Longest track on the album — establishes the scope and ambition immediately. A journey piece.
0:00 Breakbeat drums slam in at full intensity from the very first beat — no intro, maximum contrast with Track 1. Chopped Amen-style breaks, heavy processing. The silence-to-chaos transition is the album's first statement.
0:08 Filtered synth stab enters alongside the break, establishing the dark tonal center. Low-mid frequency range. Gives the drums harmonic context.
0:30 Vocal sample ("Casanova" by Baby D) drops in — becomes the hook/refrain of the track. Pitched and chopped. Used as a melodic instrument rather than lyrical content.
1:15 Additional synth layers begin stacking — rising pad, new stab pattern enters. Density increasing. ~3-4 simultaneous synth elements now.
2:00 First major arrangement peak — all elements firing. Sub-bass fully engaged. Maximum rave energy. This is the "full arrangement" state. Track will cycle between this and stripped versions.
— Breakdown Zone —
2:45 First breakdown — drums strip to half-time or drop out. Atmospheric pads and reverb take over. ~30 seconds of ambient breathing room. Synth washes and delayed fragments.
3:15 Build begins — hi-hats return, snare roll starts, filtered sweep rises. Classic rave build technique. Tension accumulates over ~15 seconds.
3:30 Second drop — full breakbeat returns with a new melodic variation. Fresh synth line introduced. The trick: same energy level, new melodic information. Keeps interest over 8+ minutes.
4:30 New section — tempo and rhythmic feel shifts subtly. Darker, more aggressive synth tones. The track evolves rather than repeats. Mid-track pivot point.
— Second Breakdown —
5:20 Extended ambient breakdown #2 — longest in the track (~45 seconds). Ethereal pads, distant vocal echoes. This is the track's emotional center. Contrast before the final assault.
6:05 Build #2 — more aggressive this time. Distorted elements, rising noise sweeps. Longer build than the first one. Tension is higher because the listener knows what's coming.
6:25 Final drop — all elements return at maximum intensity. New percussive elements added. Peak energy. The payoff. Additional percussion layers that weren't in earlier drops.
7:30 Elements begin to strip away gradually. Synths fade, leaving drums more exposed. Controlled deconstruction over ~50 seconds.
8:10 Final bars — last echoes of the break, reverb tails, fade to near-silence. Leaves sonic space for Track 3 to claim a different territory.
BPM: ~138 — Breakbeat / Hardcore
03 Their Law (feat. Pop Will Eat Itself) 6:08
Energy: Peak Industrial Rock Crossover Guitar
Album role: The political anthem. Introduces live guitars and hip-hop vocals — the album's most aggressive genre collision.
0:00 Spoken-word intro sample — political/confrontational tone. Sparse, dark ambient backdrop. ~12 seconds of voice-only with minimal texture beneath. Sets up the confrontation.
0:12 Distorted guitar riff and breakbeat drums slam in simultaneously. Heavy, industrial feel. Guitar is heavily processed — sits between electronic and rock. Bass drops from "Drop That Bassline" sample.
0:45 Vocal hook enters — shouted, aggressive delivery from PWEI. Call-and-response pattern. The vocal sits on top of the mix, deliberately raw and confrontational.
1:30 Additional synth layers and processed noise sweeps intensify the wall of sound. Track is now at ~80% density. Guitar riff continues as the anchor.
2:15 Breakdown — guitar and vocals drop out. Stripped to just beats and bass, with filtered effects. ~20 seconds. Drums become more exposed, almost hip-hop feel.
2:35 Everything crashes back in — guitar riff returns louder/harder. Vocal sample intensifies. The drop hits harder because of the stripped section. Pattern repeats with variations.
3:40 Second major breakdown — more atmospheric this time. Distant reverbed elements, ambient drift. ~30 seconds. Longer breathing room. Creates a valley before the final climb.
4:10 Build begins — rising filter sweep, drums reintroduce piece by piece. Tension builds. Snare rolls begin at 4:25, announcing the return.
4:30 Final section — all elements return at peak intensity. New guitar variation and additional vocal layers. The most intense ~90 seconds of the track. Everything at maximum.
5:45 Deconstruction begins — layers peel off over final 20 seconds. Ends on a reverb tail and low drone.
BPM: ~135 — Breakbeat / Industrial Crossover
04 Full Throttle 5:00
Energy: High Hardcore Techno Rave
Album role: Pure dancefloor energy. A more straightforward rave track — provides relief from the complexity of Tracks 2-3.
0:00 Opens with a reversed sample and hard-hitting kick drum pattern. Immediate energy. Reversed Star Wars sample ("we're going in, full throttle") — played backwards. Sets up a disorienting entry.
0:15 Full breakbeat and bass engage. Staccato synth riff establishes the main hook. More straightforward than Tracks 2-3. Direct 4/4-adjacent energy.
0:50 Vocal samples ("Hey!" from The Breeders' "SOS") layer in as rhythmic accents. Short, percussive vocal stabs. Used for texture, not melody.
1:30 Second synth layer joins — higher frequency, more insistent. Energy ramps. Film sample laughter (from A Fistful of Dollars) weaves in and out.
2:20 Breakdown — kick drops out, hi-hats and filtered elements carry momentum. ~25 seconds. Shorter breakdown than previous tracks — keeps the pace up.
2:45 Drop back in — kick returns with added sub-bass weight. New percussion pattern. Energy feels renewed rather than repeated. Fresh drum fills mark the return.
3:30 Peak section — all elements layered. Maximum density sustained for ~60 seconds.
4:30 Outro begins — elements thin out. Synths fade first, then bass, finally drums. ~30 seconds of controlled descent. Ends clean with a final reverbed hit.
BPM: ~155 — Hardcore Techno
05 Voodoo People 6:28
Energy: Peak Big Beat Guitar Riff Signature Track
Album role: The centrepiece anthem. The track that bridges electronic and rock most successfully. Iconic guitar riff (from Nirvana's "Very Ape") over relentless breakbeats.
0:00 Atmospheric intro — filtered ambient sounds, a distant warped sample, low rumble. ~20 seconds of scene-setting. Calm before the storm — alien, psychedelic texture.
0:20 The iconic guitar riff enters — clean, isolated, immediately recognizable. No drums yet. Based on Nirvana's "Very Ape," performed by Lance Riddler. Heavily treated but melodically clear.
0:35 Breakbeat drums and bass drop in beneath the riff. Full groove established. The riff-over-breaks combination is the track's DNA. Everything else layers on top.
1:10 Vocal sample enters ("The Shalimar" by Gylan Kain) — spoken-word, hypnotic, ritualistic feel. Adds the "voodoo" atmosphere. Processed with delay and reverb.
1:50 Additional synth stabs and a rising pad layer in. Track reaches first peak density. ~5-6 simultaneous elements. Guitar riff remains the constant anchor.
2:30 First breakdown — drums and bass cut. Guitar riff continues alone with ambient effects. ~20 seconds. The riff sounds completely different without the rhythm section — eerie, exposed.
2:50 Drums smash back — bigger, more distorted. New percussive elements layered. The return adds crash cymbals and a heavier kick for extra impact.
3:40 Extended peak section — all elements fully engaged. Synth leads weave around the guitar. This plateau sustains for over a minute. Pure momentum.
4:50 Second breakdown — longer, more atmospheric. Guitar drops out. Pads and distant vocal echoes. ~35 seconds. The deepest valley in the track. Creates anticipation for the finale.
5:25 Build — filtered sweep, snare rolls accelerating, guitar riff fading back in. ~15 seconds of escalating tension.
5:40 Final drop — all elements return at maximum. Additional distortion and intensity. The final 45 seconds are the track at its most unhinged.
6:10 Outro — rapid deconstruction. Elements strip in seconds. Ends on a guitar reverb trail.
BPM: ~140 — Breakbeat / Big Beat
06 Speedway (Theme from Fastlane) 7:08
Energy: High Jungle Breakbeat Cinematic
Album role: The mid-album deep cut. Longer, more hypnotic, with jungle-influenced drum patterns. Rewards close listening rather than instant gratification.
0:00 Cinematic intro — car engine/mechanical sound effects over a rising synth pad. Sets a "chase scene" atmosphere. ~15 seconds of sound design.
0:15 Rapid breakbeat enters — faster, more junglist patterns than previous tracks. Double-time feel. Hi-hats are prominent, syncopated. Drum pattern is the star.
0:45 Bass synth and a dark, circular synth melody lock in. Hypnotic groove established. Less "riff-based" than previous tracks — this is about repetition and trance state.
1:40 Textural layers — processed noise, metallic scrapes, filtered sweeps. Industrial textures add grit without changing the harmonic content.
2:30 Breakdown — drums thin to just a filtered hi-hat loop. Bass drops to sub frequencies. ~35 seconds. Minimal, hypnotic. The groove doesn't stop, it just recedes.
3:05 Full drums return with new synth pattern — higher pitch, more urgent feel. Track evolves in the second half. Melody shifts upward.
4:15 New section — additional breakbeat layer creates polyrhythmic complexity. Two break patterns overlapping. Very jungle-influenced.
5:20 Second breakdown — more atmospheric than the first. Pads and reverb dominate. ~30 seconds of spacious, almost ambient territory.
5:50 Final section — builds back with additional energy. Last ~70 seconds ride out at peak intensity.
6:50 Rapid fade. Elements collapse into silence. Brief gap before Track 7. Note: On the "More Music" reissue, over 2.5 minutes of this track continues into Track 7 — a mastering correction.
BPM: ~160 — Jungle / Breakbeat
07 The Heat (The Energy) 5:06
Energy: High Hardcore Techno Relentless
Album role: Pure kinetic energy. The most straightforwardly aggressive track — all forward momentum, minimal respite.
0:00 Immediate — hard kick drum and distorted synth stab from beat one. No preamble. Vocal sample from Poltergeist III layers in early. Horror-film tension meets dancefloor.
0:30 Breakbeat fills out beneath the kick. Layered percussion creates dense rhythmic bed. Samples from Lil Louis and Moby's "Thousand" contribute to the frantic texture.
1:15 Melodic synth element introduces — a minor-key arpeggiated line. Sits high in the mix. First real melodic content after ~75 seconds of pure rhythm.
2:00 Brief breakdown — kick drops, breakbeat becomes exposed. Film sample more prominent. Only ~15 seconds. This track doesn't give you much time to breathe.
2:15 Full return — heavier, with added low-end weight. Track reaches maximum density.
3:00 New synth pattern — more dissonant, urgent. Drives toward the track's climax. Layered over the existing arpeggiation. Harmonic tension increases.
3:45 Second breakdown — slightly longer (~20 seconds). Filtered pads and echoed vocal.
4:05 Final section — everything returns for the last minute of sustained intensity.
4:50 Abrupt ending — cuts almost dead, with a final reverb tail. Clean stop. No fade. The hard cut leaves a vacuum that Track 8 fills differently.
BPM: ~145 — Hardcore Techno
08 Poison 6:39
Energy: High Breakbeat Dark Single
Album role: The dark single. A slow-burn opener that builds into one of the album's most menacing grooves. Maxim's co-written vocal contribution.
0:00 Extended silence/near-silence — very low ambient hum. ~30 seconds of almost nothing. Deliberately provocative pacing after Track 7's relentless energy. Forces the listener to reset.
0:30 A single, heavy drum hit — then another. Sparse, deliberate rhythm emerges. Skull Snaps sample. Very slow compared to the album so far. Almost hip-hop tempo.
1:00 Bass synth creeps in — deep, venomous, pitch-bent. The "poison" feel is established. Amen Brother break begins to layer beneath. Building from the bottom up.
1:30 Vocal fragments — Maxim's processed voice. Short, menacing phrases. Not a traditional vocal performance. More like a weapon woven into the texture.
2:00 Full groove finally engages — took 2 full minutes to build. Drums at full intensity. Bass at maximum. The slow build makes this hit much harder than an immediate drop would. 2 minutes of anticipation.
2:45 Additional synth layers — dark strings, atonal stabs. Density increases. The arrangement keeps growing even after the "drop." Nothing plateaus.
3:30 Breakdown — drums strip to a minimal loop. Bass goes sub-only. Filtered, distant. ~30 seconds. Vocal sample echoes in reverb. Spacious.
4:00 Return — drums crash back. New percussive fills and a more aggressive bass pattern. The second half is darker than the first. The track gets meaner as it goes.
5:00 Peak density — all layers engaged. Maximum menace. Sustained for ~60 seconds.
6:00 Outro begins — gradual deconstruction. Elements drop one by one. Ends on bass and ambience. ~40 seconds of unwinding. Mirrors the slow-build intro.
BPM: ~128 — Breakbeat / Trip-Hop adjacent
09 No Good (Start the Dance) 6:17
Energy: High Rave House-Influenced Single
Album role: The big single / crowd-pleaser. The most accessible, "classic rave" track on the album. Provides a euphoric counterweight to the preceding darkness.
0:00 Filtered vocal sample intro — Kelly Charles' "No Good for Me" pitched and looped. Instantly iconic. Minimal backing — just the vocal and a basic filtered beat. Hook-first approach.
0:20 Kick drum and bass enter. Clean, punchy four-on-the-floor feel with breakbeat overlay. More house-influenced than the rest of the album. Funk sample from "Funky Nassau" underpins.
0:45 Full arrangement engages — synth stabs, layered percussion, vocal hook fully established. The most "classic rave" moment on the album. Immediately dancefloor-ready.
1:30 Additional synth lead — melodic, uplifting. A stark contrast to Poison's darkness. The vocal hook repeats with variation. New rhythmic accents added.
2:20 Breakdown — vocal isolated with filter effects. Drums strip to hi-hats and a distant kick. ~25 seconds. The vocal hook carries this section entirely.
2:45 Drop — full energy returns. New synth pad adds width. Bass is heavier. Standard rave drop technique but executed perfectly. The crowd-goes-wild moment.
3:30 Extended peak section — arrangement sustains maximum energy. Additional vocal chops.
4:15 Second breakdown — longer, more atmospheric. Piano/keys element briefly appears. ~30 seconds. Gives the track a second emotional arc.
4:45 Final drop — all elements return. Last 90 seconds at peak energy with subtle variations.
5:55 Outro — elements strip away. Vocal hook is the last thing to fade, reverbed into silence. The hook stays in your head because it's the last thing you hear.
BPM: ~136 — Breakbeat / Rave
10 One Love 3:53
Energy: Mid-High Rave Euphoric Edited
Album role: Transitional breather. The shortest full track — a condensed rave anthem. The "Arabic Muezzin" sample gives it a distinctive spiritual quality. Edited down from a longer original (~5:34).
0:00 Arabic vocal sample ("Muezzin" from Zero G sample CD) — ethereal, haunting, melodic. Immediately sets this apart from everything else on the album. Spiritual vs. aggressive.
0:15 Breakbeat and sub-bass enter beneath the vocal. Classic rave structure forms quickly. The edited version wastes no time. Straight to the groove.
0:35 Full arrangement — synth pads, layered percussion, vocal sample repeating. Euphoric. More uplifting than anything else on the album. A moment of transcendence.
1:20 Additional melodic synth — a rising sequence that adds urgency. Track builds continuously. No real plateau in the first half.
2:00 Breakdown — vocal sample isolated with reverb. Drums drop to minimal pattern. ~20 seconds. Short — the track's brevity demands efficiency.
2:20 Return — full energy. All elements back. New synth variation for the final section.
3:20 Outro begins — gradual fade. Vocal sample is the last element, trailing into reverb. ~30 seconds of deconstruction. Transitions into the Narcotic Suite.
BPM: ~140 — Rave / Breakbeat
11 3 Kilos (The Narcotic Suite, Part 1) 2:45
Energy: Low Downtempo Trip-Hop Ambient
Album role: The dramatic gear shift. After 10 tracks of escalating intensity, the album drops into a hazy, downtempo world. "A load of laid-back people lying around in a smoky room" — Liam Howlett.
0:00 Slow, languid beat fades in — almost hip-hop tempo. Sampled from Bernard Purdie's "Good Livin'." Drastically slower than anything before it. The tempo shift is jarring in the best way.
0:20 Deep bass and a live flute melody (Phil Bent) enter. Warm, smoky, narcotic atmosphere. The flute is a totally new sonic element for the album. Organic vs. electronic.
0:50 Additional keys/pad — warm, round tone. Vibraphone-like accents. The arrangement is sparse but rich. Every element has space.
1:30 Flute melody develops — more elaborate phrasing. Subtle rhythmic variations in the drums. The track doesn't build in intensity — it deepens in texture.
2:20 Elements begin to fade. Flute trails off. Beat thins. Crossfade into Skylined begins. No hard ending — the three Narcotic Suite tracks are designed to flow as one piece.
BPM: ~90 — Downtempo / Trip-Hop
12 Skylined (The Narcotic Suite, Part 2) 5:28
Energy: Mid Ambient Breakbeat Euphoric Uplifting
Album role: The ascent. "An uplifting, rush feel" — Liam Howlett. Tempo rises from 3 Kilos' downtempo into something more energetic but still dreamy. The emotional heart of the Narcotic Suite.
0:00 Crossfade from 3 Kilos — ambient pads and a rising synth wash. Tempo gradually increasing. The transition is seamless. New harmonic territory — brighter, more hopeful.
0:25 A breakbeat enters — mid-tempo, lighter touch than earlier tracks. More swing, less aggression. Flute continues from 3 Kilos but with a different, more uplifting melody.
1:00 Melodic synth pad — wide, warm, major-key feel. Emotional uplift. This is the album's most "beautiful" moment. Lush production.
1:45 Additional layers — strings-like synth, arpeggiated sequences. Density growing gently. The build is patient — no sudden jumps. A slow bloom.
2:30 Peak moment — all elements converge. Drums at full intensity, multiple synth layers, flute. But "full intensity" here is still softer than the album's earlier peaks. Different register.
3:15 Atmospheric section — drums recede. Pads and flute carry the track alone. ~30 seconds of floating, spacious ambience. The "skylined" feeling.
3:45 Drums return — slightly harder this time. Preparing the transition to Part 3. The mood begins to darken subtly. Harmonic content shifts toward minor keys.
4:45 Tonal shift — brighter elements fade. Darker, more ominous textures seep in. The transition to Claustrophobic Sting is happening in real time.
5:10 Final bars — most elements gone. A low, unsettling drone remains. Crossfade to Track 13. The beauty of Skylined is replaced by dread. The Suite's emotional arc in action.
BPM: ~120 rising to ~130 — Ambient Breakbeat
13 Claustrophobic Sting (The Narcotic Suite, Part 3) 7:13
Energy: Peak (Dark) Dark Ambient Industrial Cinematic
Album role: The apocalyptic closer. "A paranoid, depths-of-hell track, probably the most forbidding music I've ever written" — Liam Howlett. Ends the album in darkness. Contains a recreation of dialogue from 2001: A Space Odyssey.
0:00 Crossfade from Skylined — a dark, cavernous drone. Sub-bass rumble. Oppressive atmosphere. The "claustrophobic" sensation starts immediately. Spatial compression.
0:30 Slow, pounding kick drum — very sparse, each hit reverbed into a massive space. Almost processional. Like footsteps in a cathedral. ~80 BPM.
1:00 Industrial textures — metallic scrapes, distorted mechanical sounds. No melody. Pure texture and rhythm. Unsettling sound design.
1:45 2001: A Space Odyssey dialogue recreation appears — processed, distant, eerie. Adds a sci-fi/existential dimension. The album ends not in rave, but in space.
2:30 Breakbeat enters — but darker, more distorted than anywhere on the album. Drums sound damaged, corroded. Filtered and bit-crushed.
3:15 Bass drops — massive, sub-heavy. Combined with the distorted breaks, creates a wall of darkness. This is the album's deepest, most claustrophobic moment. Maximum density, minimum light.
4:00 Additional noise layers — rising dissonance, feedback-like tones. The track becomes progressively more unhinged. Controlled chaos.
4:45 Breakdown — but not relief. Drums drop, but the ambient dread intensifies. ~30 seconds of pure horror-soundtrack ambience. The darkness is louder than the beats.
5:15 Final assault — all elements return at maximum darkness. Distortion, sub-bass, industrial percussion. The last ~90 seconds of peak intensity. No redemption arc — the album ends in the depths.
6:30 Deconstruction begins — elements strip away slowly. Reverb tails extend into nothing. The final 40 seconds are a slow fade into void. Sub-bass is the last audible element.
7:05 Near-silence. A final low hum, then nothing. The album ends. The last few seconds mirror the album's opening — ambient nothingness bookending 78 minutes of chaos.
BPM: ~80 rising to ~135 — Dark Ambient / Industrial

Album-Level Arc — The Macro Structure

Act I — The Statement

Opens with a manifesto, then hits with the album's longest and most ambitious track. Establishes scope, ambition, and the rock/electronic fusion. Three tracks of escalating genre collision.

Tracks 1–3 · 15:18 total · ~20% of album

Act II — The Dancefloor

Four tracks of high-energy rave and breakbeat, each with distinct character. Moves from hardcore (Full Throttle) through jungle (Speedway) to the album's darkest (Poison) and most accessible (No Good) moments.

Tracks 4–9 · 36:38 total · ~47% of album

Act III — The Comedown

One Love acts as a bridge — euphoric but brief. Then the Narcotic Suite takes the listener on a three-part descent: hazy calm → emotional uplift → apocalyptic darkness. The album ends at its darkest.

Tracks 10–13 · 19:19 total · ~25% of album

Energy Shape

The overall energy curve is: silence → explosion → sustained high with peaks/valleys → brief euphoria → downtempo → beautiful rise → dark collapse. It mirrors the arc of a night out: arrival, peak, comedown, aftermath.

Key: Tracks 2, 5, 8 are the three main peaks

BPM Journey

The tempos are not linear. They surge and retreat: 0 → 138 → 135 → 155 → 140 → 160 → 145 → 128 → 136 → 140 → 90 → 120 → 80-135. The big dip at Track 8 (Poison) resets the floor before the Narcotic Suite's collapse.

Range: 80–160 BPM · Median ~138

Blueprint Takeaways

Open with a sub-1-minute manifesto. Follow with your most ambitious piece. Alternate between rock-fusion aggression and pure dancefloor energy. Place your darkest single at the 2/3 mark. End with a multi-part suite that moves from calm to beautiful to devastating.
