Equinoxe

Artist: Jean-Michel Jarre
Released: December 1978
Total Length: 39:02
Tracks: 8
Producer: Jean-Michel Jarre

Overview

Equinoxe is a conceptual day cycle — eight parts mapping the progression from pre-dawn to late night. Recorded again in Jarre's Paris apartment, it expands the Oxygène palette with new equipment: Yamaha CS-60 (the warm polysynth central to the daytime sections), Oberheim Polyphonic synthesizer, Geiss Matrisequencer 250 (an upgraded sequencer from the Rhytmi-computer), Korg KR 55 (more sophisticated drum machine than the Mini-Pops 7), and EMS Vocoder 1000. Same recording team: Jarre, Michel Geiss on technical assistance, Jean-Pierre Janiaud on engineering.

Used in Carl Sagan's Cosmos (1980). A 1 million–person audience attended the Place de la Concorde concert in July 1979. UK #11, France #1. ~3 million worldwide. Part 4 is the midday peak; Part 7 is the longest track (7:24) and the album's most cinematic piece. Timestamps approximate to ±5 seconds.

02 Equinoxe Part 2 5:01
Energy: Mid Morning Sequenced Rhythm Geiss Matrisequencer
Album role: Morning arrives — the day's first energy. A rising, purposeful sequence announces the working start of the day. The Geiss Matrisequencer 250's tighter, more complex patterns replace the Mini-Pops 7 in the driving role.

The Yamaha CS-60 (1977) is a polyphonic analog synthesizer with individual voice architecture. Its filter has a rounder, warmer character than the ARP 2600.
2:30 Layering — multiple voices active — the morning fills with activity. The concept begins to assert itself beyond mere mood-setting. The day has structure; Part 2 maps that structure's beginning.
3:45 Morning peak — full arrangement at maximum morning energy. Jarre doesn't overplay Part 2. He reserves maximum energy for Part 4 — midday.
4:30 Transition toward Part 3 — energy sustained but beginning to develop into the more complex late-morning material of the adjacent section. On the original vinyl, Parts 1–4 run on Side One with no gap.

Album role: Late morning — more complex, slightly busier than Part 2. The Korg KR 55 drum machine (more elaborate than the Mini-Pops 7) adds a denser rhythmic layer. The day is well underway; this is mid-morning at peak activity before the midday summit.

0:45 Korg KR 55 pattern — the more sophisticated drum machine provides a busier, more detailed rhythmic texture than the Mini-Pops. The KR 55 allows more complex programmed patterns. The late-morning soundscape has more moving parts than the clean morning launch of Part 2.

4:30 Peak of Part 3 — Side One's secondary summit — not the album's highest point but Part 3's own internal apex. Immediately gives way to Part 4. The two climaxes are adjacent — Part 3's ending merges into Part 4's opening.

04 Equinoxe Part 4 6:52
Energy: Peak Midday Climax Side One Apex EMS Vocoder 1000
Album role: Midday — the album's first and highest energy climax. Part 4 is the summit of the day and of the entire album's first half. The most assertive, driven, and rhythmically complex section on Side One — where the sun is at its zenith and the album at its most awake. Longest track on Side One.
0:00 Full midday energy launch — maximum rhythmic complexity from Part 4's first moment. No build: this is already the peak. The Korg KR 55 and Matrisequencer at their busiest. After the building arc of Parts 1–3, Part 4 arrives fully formed.
0:50 EMS Vocoder 1000 — the vocoder processes and transforms vocal or instrument sounds into synthetic voice textures. The EMS Vocoder 1000 had been used to create alien or robotic voice effects since Kraftwerk popularised the device. Jarre uses it textural rather than as a lead voice.
2:00 Layering continues toward internal peak — the Yamaha CS-60 melody is most prominent here, riding over the complex rhythm. Part 4's golden, driven quality — the warmth of the CS-60 over insistent percussion — is the album's most commercially resonant moment.
3:30 Maximum complexity — the album's peak moment (Side One) — all instruments contributing simultaneously. The Place de la Concorde concert audience of 1 million heard this moment under open sky. Their applause between sections is on the live album.
5:00 Post-peak reduction — the midday sun begins its descent. Energy released gradually. The back half of Part 4 is the afternoon arriving — Side Two will map the second half of the day.
6:20 Side Two transition — the energy drops to a sustained level, preparing the listener for the flip. Side One ends at the midday summit's decline. Side Two picks up in mid-afternoon.
BPM: ~124 — Electronic / Maximum Intensity
— Side Two: Afternoon to Deep Night —
05 Equinoxe Part 5 3:54
Energy: Mid Afternoon Yamaha CS-60 Post-Peak Warmth
Album role: Afternoon warmth after the midday peak — the day's energy declining from its zenith but still full of light. Part 5 is gentler than Part 4 but retains its forward motion. Transitional in the day-arc from the activity of midday to the quieter late afternoon of Part 6.
0:00 Afternoon register — reduced intensity — warmth without the midday urgency. The CS-60 carries the melody with a more relaxed character. Side Two begins at a deliberately lower energy level than Part 4 ended. The sun is past its peak.
1:00 Melodic warmth develops — the afternoon's golden light in sonic form. CS-60 polysynth at its most lyrical. The Yamaha CS-60's warm filter character is most appropriate for this time-of-day — richer and fuller than morning, softer than midday.
2:30 Part 5's modest peak — contained, unhurried. This is not a second midday but an afternoon that knows its place. Structural self-awareness: Part 5 refuses to compete with Part 4. Its role is warmth, not drama.
3:20 Gradual release into Part 6 — the afternoon fades toward evening. The day is now past its productive centre. Parts 6–8 map the transition into night.
BPM: ~114 — Electronic / Warm
06 Equinoxe Part 6 3:15
Energy: Low Early Evening Transitional
Album role: Early evening — the briefest track on Side Two. A transitional section bridging the afternoon warmth of Part 5 to the nocturnal epic of Part 7. As the light fades, the music becomes more introspective and less rhythmically insistent.
0:00 Evening palette shift — cooler tones, slower harmonic movement. The temperature has dropped. The transition from afternoon warmth to evening cool is achieved through tonal register more than tempo change.
1:20 Introspective melodic turn — the melody becomes more inward-looking than the daytime sections. Part 6 is the doorway. On one side: the sunny afternoon of Parts 4–5. On the other: the night epic of Part 7.
2:50 Evening dissolves toward night — texture thins. Part 7's opening waits. Three minutes is enough to shift the album's psychological register from day to night.
07 Equinoxe Part 7 7:24
Energy: High Night Epic Longest Track Most Cinematic Carl Sagan Cosmos
Album role: The album's nocturnal centrepiece and most cinematic piece — the longest track at 7:24. Night reverses the day's journey: where the day built toward midday from a quiet dawn, the night builds toward a different, darker climax. Used in Carl Sagan's Cosmos (1980) — the piece that gave Equinoxe its most prominent cultural placement.
0:00 Night opens — the darkest, most expansive texture on the album. Stars rather than sun. After the compressed brevity of Part 6, Part 7 opens the space of the album to its maximum. Night has more room than day.
1:00 Night melody develops — slower, more deliberate than the daytime sequences. Cosmic rather than solar. Carl Sagan selected this piece for Cosmos — the universe as experienced from a synthesizer room at night. An unexpectedly perfect match.
2:30 Layering nocturnal depth — dense low frequencies, atmospheric washes. The night in full dimension. The album's equipment range at its most dramatic: EMS Synthi AKS for unpredictable textural movement; ARP 2600 for the deeper bass sequences.
4:00 Night peak — the album's second major climax — Part 7 reaches its summit. Darker and more dramatic than Part 4's midday brightness; this is the energy of the night sky rather than the sun. Two peaks: Part 4 (midday, bright, rhythmically assertive) and Part 7 (midnight, vast, harmonically dramatic). The day and night halves of the album mirror each other.
5:30 Night deconstruction — the peak energy releases. Post-midnight. The night has done its work. The album moves toward Part 8's resolution — late night into the quiet before dawn.
6:45 Approaching Part 8 — the night's last movement. Fatigue and quiet. Part 7 earns its length by covering the most emotional territory. Its 7:24 is the album's most demanding and rewarding single experience.
BPM: ~104 — Night / Electronic Cinematic
08 Equinoxe Part 8 5:02
Energy: Low Late Night Resolution Day Cycle Complete
Album role: Late night / pre-dawn — the album completes its 24-hour cycle. Part 8 mirrors Part 1: both are atmospheric and spare, book-ending the day. The album ends where it began — not quite day, not quite night. The equinox: equal darkness and light.
0:00 Late night atmosphere — sparse, tired, still. The day is completely done. The mirror of Part 1: both short (relative to the expansive middle sections), both atmospheric, both near the day's boundary.
1:30 Quiet melody in the dark — a single melodic voice, soft and resigned. The synthesizers that were assertive in Parts 2–4 are here patient and understated. The same instruments at rest.
3:00 Final harmonic movement — slow chord progression that the entire 39-minute album has been approaching. The resolution is harmonically simple but emotionally satisfying after the journey of the preceding seven parts.
4:20 Dissolution — the equinox completes — the last sounds fade. Day and night equalised. The album ends not in silence but in the same atmospheric near-silence as its beginning. The cycle is complete. Tomorrow, Part 1 begins again.

Macro Arc — Album Structure

Side One: Dawn to Midday

Parts 1–4 trace the arc from pre-dawn stillness (1: 2:23), through morning energy (2–3), to the midday peak (4: 6:52). Side One is the ascending half — four movements that build from near-silence to maximum energy.

Parts 1–4 · ~20:27

Side Two: Afternoon to Night

Parts 5–8 map the day's descent — afternoon warmth (5), early evening (6), the night epic (7, the album's centrepiece), and late-night resolution (8). The album doesn't return to dawn's emptiness but rests in a deeper, more resolved night.

Parts 5–8 · ~19:35

Two Climaxes: Sun & Night

Part 4 (midday) and Part 7 (night) are the album's two climaxes — structurally paired but characteristically opposite. Part 4 is bright, rhythmically driven, and golden. Part 7 is dark, vast, and cinematic. The equinox: equal and opposed.

Parts 4 & 7

New Equipment: 1978 Palette

Yamaha CS-60 (warm polysynth — the daytime voice), Oberheim Polyphonic (brighter, more precise), Geiss Matrisequencer 250 (more complex sequencer patterns), Korg KR 55 (fuller drum patterns), EMS Vocoder 1000 (alien voice texture). The apartment kitchen now contains a larger studio.
